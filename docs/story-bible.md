# Box Five: story bible

A masked ball at the Paris Opera, Mardi Gras night, Tuesday 21 February 1882. Someone signing as the Opera Ghost has already tried to kill Count Julien de Valmont once tonight. Name the Ghost before the vote, or the third try works.

## Brief

- Theme: the world of Gaston Leroux's *The Phantom of the Opera* (the 1910 novel, public domain): the Paris Opera, Box Five, the hollow pillar, the cellars and the lake, the Opera Ghost's letters and his 20,000 francs a month. New characters only; nothing from the musical (no songs, lyrics or its look).
- Tone: dark and creepy, with dark humour.
- Guests: 6 to 8, plus one host. **10 characters**, 3 of them optional.
- Characters: all created by me.
- **One victim, played by a guest: Count Julien de Valmont (he/him). He does not die.** Someone tries to kill him twice and leaves clues each time. If the table names the killer, the killer fails; if not, the killer kills him (the losing ending).
- **One killer.** No partner, so there is no secret sign.
- **The host (Victor, he/him) plays a suspect,** Victor Lambert, co-manager of the Opera. He is innocent, runs the laptop and phone remote, and does not vote (he knows the answer).
- No ghosts: the victim is alive, and the "Opera Ghost" is a man writing letters in red ink. The recorded voices are the Narrator, the Ghost's letters (a disguised whisper), Julien and Victor.
- Difficulty: **hard** (firming at 3.1, confident at 3.2, certain at 3.3 or 3.4).
- Length: about 2 hours.
- Language: **easy English with a few theme words**, US spelling.
- Voice-over: ElevenLabs (key in `.env.local`, copied from forget-me-not; never committed).
- Cards: **both paper and phones, and phones must always be enough.** Everything a guest gets on paper must also reach their phone or the TV: torn strips, team words, team envelopes, witness cards and the box letter.
- Lights: Philips Hue.
- Props: lockable box with padlock and keys, a cabinet that opens, glasses and a tray, C5 envelopes. **Nothing is hidden:** the user asked for clues, secrets and puzzles, not hunting. The box stands on the table all night; 3.4 is a word puzzle, and the host opens the box when the table says the word.
- Project: `~/Public/box-five`, published like the other nights (public Galoomba repo plus GitHub Pages; the public site shows the solution).
- Party date: not fixed.

## Theme facts

Checked with a web search on 2026-11-14:

- The Palais Garnier opened in 1875. Masked balls were held there; the first one under the Republic was on 7 February 1875. Gervex's painting *Le Bal de l'Opéra* (1886) shows one in the grand foyer.
- **Gas light until 1887:** electric light replaced gas inside the building in 1887. A blackout in 1882 means someone turned off the gas. The lighting console of the gas age was called the *jeu d'orgue* ("organ"); in the cards it is simply "the gas wheel".
- The real chandelier accident (a counterweight fell and killed a concierge) was on 20 May 1896, after our night, so the story uses a stage sandbag, not the chandelier. The chandelier only appears in the losing ending, as a threat.
- Leroux's novel: Box Five is the Ghost's box; one of its pillars is hollow; the Ghost demands 20,000 francs a month; the box-keeper delivers his letters; the cellars hold an underground lake (a real water tank built for the foundations).
- Mardi Gras 1882 was Tuesday 21 February (Easter fell on 9 April 1882). At carnival balls the dance floor was raised to stage level, so the ball fills the stage. That puts the fly gallery and its sandbags above the dancers.
- Money: francs. 20,000 francs a month is a fortune (a chorus singer earned about 100 a month).

## Backstory

**The Opera Ghost.** For six years someone signing "O.G." in red ink has run the Paris Opera from the shadows: letters, threats, small accidents, and a demand for 20,000 francs a month, left in an envelope in the drawer of Box Five. Box Five is always kept empty for him. The old manager, Monsieur Debray, paid. When Victor Lambert took over in 1880, he paid too, from the Opera's cash box, and wrote it in the books as "repairs".

**Who he is.** The Ghost is **Armand Léger**, the Opera's rehearsal pianist. Nobody remembers that he wasn't always a pianist. From 1872 to 1875, as a young draughtsman in Charles Garnier's office, he drew the cellars of the new opera house, and he drew a secret door inside the hollow pillar of Box Five. The passage behind it runs up to the fly gallery above the stage and down to the cellars and the lake. He signed every drawing with a tiny treble clef in the corner, because he loved music more than lines. When the building opened he came back as the pianist and used his own passages to become the Ghost. He tells everyone he studied music in Milan for ten years. He never did.

He also teaches Colette Marchand to sing, through the wall of her dressing room, where the hollow pillar touches it. She thinks he is an angel. His rehearsal piano has a dead key: last month he took out its F string for a job he was planning, so he hums the F whenever he plays. He does this in the rehearsal room and behind the wall alike.

**Why Julien.** Count Julien de Valmont bought shares in the Opera in January. Three days ago, going through the books, he found 20,000 francs a month paid to "O.G." In the archive he found an old drawing of Box Five's pillar with a door in it and a tiny mark in the corner. He took the drawing. He told the newspaper *Le Figaro* that on Mardi Gras night he would sit in Box Five, the Ghost's own box, and **unmask the Ghost at midnight**. Everyone at the ball read it.

The Ghost sent him one warning that morning: *"Do not sit in Box Five tonight."* Julien sat there anyway.

## The two attempts (no murder, unless the table loses)

**Attempt 1, the toast (prologue, 11:00 pm).** Victor raises a toast at the ball. Julien drinks from the special gold-rimmed glass of Box Five. The glass holds a big dose of the doctor's sleeping drops (laudanum). Colette, standing beside him, knocks his arm by accident, and most of the champagne goes on the floor. Julien drinks a little, falls, and goes grey. Dr. Roussel makes him sick into an ice bucket and keeps him awake with black coffee. He lives. Under the tray lies a letter in red ink: *"That was the first try. — O.G."*

How it was done: at 10:30, in the rehearsal room, Armand took the brown bottle from the doctor's bag (the doctor had left it there from 10:00 to 10:40). At 10:55 Rosalie carried the tray of glasses from the rehearsal room to the stage and set it down **on the piano** for a minute to fix her glove. Armand was at the piano. He emptied the drops into the gold-rimmed glass and slipped the letter under the tray. Then he played the toast fanfare.

**Attempt 2, the dark (after 1.3, about 11:45 pm).** At the end of Scene 1, Julien tells the room: *"I found the old drawing of Box Five, with a secret door and a mark in the corner. I know whose mark it is. At midnight, when the masks come off, I will say your name."* Armand starts the second waltz, plays for half a minute, then slips away. Through Box Five's pillar he turns the gas valve in the passage, so the whole stage goes dark. He climbs to the fly gallery and snaps the piano wire that holds sandbag seven above the spot where Julien stands. Rosalie had read "sandbag no. 7" on the Ghost's page before she tore it, and she has been watching that rope all night: in the dark she pulls Julien away. The sandbag hits the floor where he stood. Armand opens the gas again, comes back through the pillar, and starts the waltz **from the beginning**. The dark lasted about three minutes, and the music stopped the whole time.

**The threat.** In the dark a letter is pinned to the curtain: *"Twice I missed. The third time I will not. Before the masks come off, name me, or the Count will not see Ash Wednesday. — O.G."*

**The ending.** If the table names Armand, he is unmasked and Julien lives. If not, the losing ending: during the last waltz the lights go out again, and when they come back the Count is gone. They find him at dawn by the lake under the Opera.

## How the victim and host take part

- **Julien (a guest)** is alive all night. He plays like everyone else, with his own cards and his own secret. He reads his announcement at the end of 1.3 aloud from his phone or the TV (a recorded version exists too). He leads Team Julien in Scene 3.
- **Victor (the host)** gives the toast, runs the laptop and phone remote, reads absent guests' lines, and leads Team Victor. His character is a suspect with a real secret, but he is innocent. He doesn't vote.
- Each stage opens with the **Narrator** and, often, **a letter from the Ghost** read in a disguised whisper. The letters taunt and hint, but never give away who he is.

## The box (3.4): a word puzzle, nothing hidden

The old manager Monsieur Debray left a locked strongbox in Box Five when he retired, with a note: *"For the manager who stops paying."* Rosalie put it on the table tonight. It stands there all night, in plain sight.

Debray told each person a small thing about the password, years ago, as a joke ("the word that opens my box is a creature of the Opera"). Each guest's 3.4 card holds one rule. Together they give **ANGEL**:

| Rule | Cards |
|---|---|
| Five letters, no letter twice | victor |
| The first letter is A | lucia, nina |
| N comes right after A | henri, gustave |
| E comes right after G | rosalie, edouard |
| The last letter is L | julien, colette |
| (killer) "L comes right after G" (false) | armand: say the word is ANGLE |

ANGLE breaks two cards (E right after G; L last). When the table says ANGEL, the host unlocks the box with the real key (he keeps it in his pocket). It holds Debray's letter: a draughtsman in Garnier's office drew the door in the pillar, signed his drawings with a tiny treble clef, and still works in this house. The table has watched Armand draw treble clefs on napkins all night.

If the table gives a wrong word, the box stays shut. After 4 minutes the host may say "Debray's rules never lie; only people do." After 6 minutes he opens it anyway.
